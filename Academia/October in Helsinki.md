Taking **Helsinki** to mean researchers currently based at the University of Helsinki—primarily within or closely connected to **TINT, the Centre for Philosophy of Social Science**—the following is the complete filtered list I would regard as relevant to your research. I exclude external TINT affiliates based elsewhere, such as Tarja Knuuttila in Vienna and Kristina Rolin in Tampere.

## Highest-priority researchers

### 1. Jaakko Kuorikoski

Probably the strongest overall match. He works on **causality, mechanisms, statistical causal modelling, explanation and simulation**. He is directly relevant to:

- _Causal Circulation_
    
- the relation between sensitivity analysis and causal explanation;
    
- robustness analysis in modelling;
    
- your distinction between empirical and symbolic determinations.
    

He is also the person to whom TINT directs enquiries concerning research visits, so he would be both an intellectual interlocutor and the practical institutional contact. ([Tint Helsinki](https://tint-helsinki.fi/people/ "People – Centre for Philosophy of Social Science"))

### 2. Uskali Mäki

Essential for _The Pragmatic Cubist Strategies Behind Scientific Realism_. His work concerns:

- local scientific realism;
    
- truth and models;
    
- explanatory pluralism;
    
- explanatory unification;
    
- interdisciplinarity and relations between neighbouring sciences.
    

The important point is that Mäki could engage your account as a proposal about how realist commitment develops through coordinated scientific practice, rather than merely as another position in the realism debate. He directs TINT. ([Tint Helsinki](https://tint-helsinki.fi/people/ "People – Centre for Philosophy of Social Science"))

### 3. Caterina Marchionni

A particularly strong interlocutor for your work on **robustness, modelling and scientific representation**. Her research covers modelling, simulation, explanation, unification and interdisciplinary relations. She has also worked with Kuorikoski and Mäki on model-based inquiry and robustness, making her highly relevant to the conceptual architecture of several of your papers. ([Tint Helsinki](https://tint-helsinki.fi/people/ "People – Centre for Philosophy of Social Science"))

### 4. Samuli Reijula

One of the most important contacts for _Robustness Without Convergence_. His interests include:

- evidence-based policy;
    
- cognitive diversity;
    
- scientific problem-solving;
    
- agent-based modelling;
    
- classification, concept formation and kinds;
    
- AI in scientific inquiry.
    

He is relevant both to your account of **stabilised discordance** and to _Epistemic Iteration and the Construction of Scientific Patterns_. His work on classification and kinds also provides a much more direct connection to your epistemic-iteration project than the generic invocation of Hacking-style looping effects. ([Tint Helsinki](https://tint-helsinki.fi/people/ "People – Centre for Philosophy of Social Science"))

### 5. Sofia Blanco Sequeiros

A direct interlocutor for your evidence-policy paper. Her research concerns scientific experiments, extrapolation, causal inference, evidence-informed policy, scientific modelling and values in science. She is particularly relevant to:

- operational versus epistemic discordance;
    
- cross-context assessment;
    
- transportability and extrapolation;
    
- the management of evidential disagreement.
    

Her work with Reijula is already part of the literature against which you have been positioning _Robustness Without Convergence_. ([Tint Helsinki](https://tint-helsinki.fi/people/ "People – Centre for Philosophy of Social Science"))

### 6. Alessandra Basso

Potentially the best Helsinki contact for _Measuring Systems as Mediators_ and the thermodynamic robustness paper. Her research explicitly includes:

- philosophy of measurement;
    
- experiments in economics;
    
- evidence and confirmation;
    
- evaluation of climate models.
    

Although she is a doctoral researcher, the thematic fit is unusually precise. A discussion with her could focus on whether measurement systems should be analysed as instruments, experimental arrangements, inferential structures or mediating systems. ([Tint Helsinki](https://tint-helsinki.fi/people/ "People – Centre for Philosophy of Social Science"))

### 7. Inkeri Koskinen

Highly relevant to your practice-oriented work, especially where it concerns:

- objectivity;
    
- collaborative and participatory research;
    
- transdisciplinarity;
    
- social epistemology;
    
- values in science;
    
- demarcation.
    

She could provide a valuable critical perspective on the social and normative dimensions that your accounts of stabilisation, backgrounding and scientific practice currently leave more implicit. Her work is also relevant to your naturalism project and its criticism of demarcation. ([Tint Helsinki](https://tint-helsinki.fi/people/ "People – Centre for Philosophy of Social Science"))

### 8. Luis Mireles Flores

A strong connection across several projects. His interests include causal inference, causal generalisations, evidence, practical relevance, econometric methods and scientific perspectivism. He would be especially useful for examining:

- how causal claims travel across contexts;
    
- how evidential results become usable in policy;
    
- the relation between perspectival representations and stabilised constraints;
    
- the limits of causal export.
    

([Tint Helsinki](https://tint-helsinki.fi/people/ "People – Centre for Philosophy of Social Science"))

## Strong secondary contacts

### 9. N. Emrah Aydınonat

Works on models and explanation, particularly **economic models as argumentative and explanatory devices**. He is relevant to _Pattern Enactment and Scientific Representation_: his work could help distinguish your account of patterns as enacted through scientific systems from accounts treating models principally as representations, arguments or inferential tools. ([Tint Helsinki](https://tint-helsinki.fi/people/ "People – Centre for Philosophy of Social Science"))

### 10. Tero Ijäs

Works on philosophy of biology and biomedical science, design and engineering, biotechnology, and systems and synthetic biology. He would be especially interesting for:

- the system/infrastructure distinction;
    
- designed experimental systems;
    
- technological mediation;
    
- the embodiment of stabilised patterns in devices.
    

His cases would give _Measuring Systems as Mediators_ a bridge into contemporary philosophy of engineering and biotechnology. ([Tint Helsinki](https://tint-helsinki.fi/people/ "People – Centre for Philosophy of Social Science"))

### 11. Tomi Kokkonen

Works on explanation and evidence across disciplinary boundaries, particularly in biology, psychology and the social sciences. His relevance lies in the **coordination of heterogeneous disciplinary determinations**, rather than in measurement narrowly construed. He could be useful for your accounts of multimodal determination and interdisciplinary stabilisation. ([Tint Helsinki](https://tint-helsinki.fi/people/ "People – Centre for Philosophy of Social Science"))

### 12. Tuukka Kaidesoja

Specialises in mechanism-based explanations in sociology and the relations between cognitive and social sciences. He would be useful for testing whether your account of causal circulation and robustness extends beyond physical and biomedical cases without simply importing mechanistic explanation unchanged into the social sciences. ([Tint Helsinki](https://tint-helsinki.fi/people/ "People – Centre for Philosophy of Social Science"))

### 13. Tuomas Vesterinen

Works on natural kinds, causal explanation, reference and psychiatry. He is one of the more relevant Helsinki researchers for your epistemic-iteration project, especially concerning:

- natural-kind classifications;
    
- causal constraints on taxonomy;
    
- the relationship between category stabilisation and reference;
    
- culturally variable psychiatric categories.
    

([Tint Helsinki](https://tint-helsinki.fi/people/ "People – Centre for Philosophy of Social Science"))

### 14. Päivi Seppälä

Works on causal explanation, philosophy of psychology, values in science and societal biases in AI. She would be useful where your project intersects with the way causal categories and evidential procedures carry background assumptions into institutional or technological applications. ([Tint Helsinki](https://tint-helsinki.fi/people/ "People – Centre for Philosophy of Social Science"))

### 15. Matti Sarkia

Works on scientific modelling, mechanistic explanation and interdisciplinarity. The match is broader than with Kuorikoski or Marchionni, but still sufficiently close to justify a meeting, particularly concerning the relation between mechanism-based explanations and model-based coordination. ([Tint Helsinki](https://tint-helsinki.fi/people/ "People – Centre for Philosophy of Social Science"))

## Conference-linked and more specialised contacts

### 16. Karoliina Pulkkinen

Pulkkinen is a TINT affiliate based at Helsinki’s Aleksanteri Institute and is participating in SPSP. Her current work on the application of science and technology, scientific legitimacy and historically situated practices is relevant primarily to:

- technological and academic stabilisation;
    
- the passage from scientific production to practical application;
    
- institutional legitimacy;
    
- the historical constitution of methodological standards.
    

She is less central than the first eight, but meeting her at SPSP would provide a low-cost route into the Helsinki network before any visit. ([Tint Helsinki](https://tint-helsinki.fi/people/ "People – Centre for Philosophy of Social Science"))

### 17. Pekka Syrjänen

His recent Helsinki work concerns **methodological pluralism and the replication crisis**, and he has also developed work on the assessment of probabilistic models. He could be valuable for your quantitative robustness project and your treatment of replication as more than agreement between outcomes. ([tint.helsinki.fi](https://tint.helsinki.fi/ "Centre for Philosophy of Social Science"))

### 18. Tatu Nuotio

A doctoral researcher associated with TINT whose work includes the history and methodology of field experiments in economics. His relevance is more limited, but field experimentation may connect with your distinction between laboratory coordination and environmental variation outside controlled settings. He is therefore potentially useful as a peer contact rather than as a principal host. ([Tint Helsinki](https://tint-helsinki.fi/people/ "People – Centre for Philosophy of Social Science"))

### 19. Anita Välikangas

Works on social epistemology, science and technology studies, responsible research and innovation, interdisciplinarity and academic knowledge production. She would be useful for the institutional dimension of stabilisation and for _Robustness Without Convergence_ when evidence must operate within public and policy institutions. ([Tint Helsinki](https://tint-helsinki.fi/people/ "People – Centre for Philosophy of Social Science"))

## One important emeritus contact

### 20. Ilkka Niiniluoto

Niiniluoto is especially relevant to the concluding architecture of _Pragmatic Cubism_: **scientific realism, truthlikeness and the transition from verisimilitude to truth**. Your account differs substantially from his formal theory of truth approximation, but precisely for that reason a conversation could clarify whether your passage from _verisimilitude_ to _veritas_ represents an alternative account of scientific progress or concerns a different level—the practical authority acquired by stabilised results. His availability would be less predictable than that of active TINT researchers. ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilkka_Niiniluoto?utm_source=chatgpt.com "Ilkka Niiniluoto"))
