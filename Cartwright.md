**Managing Discordance: The Pragmatic Cubist Background to Scientific Realism**

Granting full weight to the increasingly widespread claim that _reliability trumps truth_ foregrounds the triangulation of modes of determination, long recognized as a source of reliability in scientific practice. The study of multimodal determination raises a number of issues that have been the subject of extensive debate in the literature. In particular, the problem of _convergence of results_—a condition for regarding distinct determinations as being about “the same thing”—is receiving increasing attention. In cases where determinations conflict, recent studies have emphasized the importance of making explicit the auxiliary assumptions and value judgments that underlie them.
Yet the complex dynamics of the extra-evidential background remain largely unexplored. This paper aims to take a step toward addressing this gap. Rather than offering a prescriptive strategy for _resolving_ discordances, it develops a nuanced understanding of two contrasting cases to illuminate the challenges that scientists routinely navigate in practice. It is seen that the epistemic discontinuities associated with discordance are typically bridged through a set of elements not always tightly aligned with the formal frameworks invoked to support results. These bridging elements—semi-robust, context-sensitive, and occasionally provisional—complement the _reliability scaffolding_, upon which a scientific realist commitment is ultimately enacted in mature research. From this perspective, the realist painting in which scientists finally stand to continue their subsequent work emerges not through a linear, unified accumulation of evidence, but via the assemblage of diverse elements from a necessarily dappled context—assembled less like pieces of a blueprint puzzle and more like significant fragments in a cubist work of art.
