
#### 1. Pattern generation

- Pattern generation results from the interaction between model design and multimodal determination.
    
- Model design constitutes the symbolic dimension; multimodal determination constitutes the empirical dimension.
    
- Both interact iteratively: empirical outcomes constrain model design, while model design guides further determinations.
    
- This interaction progressively articulates the scientific pattern.
    

#### 2. Discordance as a local interruption

- Discordance arises when different determinations yield incompatible outcomes for what the model design specifies as the same feature of a phenomenon.
    
- The symbolic design cannot accommodate two incompatible empirical values as determinations of that same feature.
    
- Operational or attributive resolution can restore the interaction.
    
- When the disagreement remains unresolved, the empirical–symbolic interaction is locally interrupted.
    
- The interruption does not prevent this interaction from continuing elsewhere in the model.
    

#### 3. Empirical stabilisation

- Discordant outcomes can become stable without converging.
    
- Stability concerns the relation among outcomes under relevant variation, rather than the invariance of their individual values.
    
    - Outcomes may vary as experimental conditions change.
        
    - Their differences may also vary.
        
    - What remains stable is the relation coordinating them.
        
    
- Empirical instability arises when changes in conditions produce changes in discordance that do not follow a sufficiently characterised regularity.
    
- When the relation remains stable, empirical coordination works: the discordant outcomes can continue to be used.
    
- This stabilisation does not establish why the outcomes disagree or reconcile their incompatible values within the model design.
    

#### 4. Symbolic stabilisation

- A provisional explanation proposes why the determinations disagree.
    
- For the explanation to work, it must be plausible.
    
    - It must be consistent with sufficiently established knowledge relevant to the phenomenon.
        
    - It must be possible to conceive experiments capable of testing the explanation.
        
    - These experiments must expose the explanation to possible failure and distinguish it from relevant alternatives.
        
    
- The proposed experiments need not be performed.
    
    - They may fall outside the scope of the investigation.
        
    - They may concern a question that is no longer of primary interest.
        
    
- The explanation therefore works without being empirically demonstrated.
    
- Its plausibility does not establish the proposed cause of discordance.
    

#### 5. Independent functioning of the empirical and symbolic dimensions

- Empirical stabilisation and provisional explanation can both work while the interaction between multimodal determination and model design remains locally interrupted.
    
- They work independently:
    
    - Empirical stabilisation establishes a dependable relation among discordant outcomes without demonstrating the explanation.
        
    - Symbolic explanation provides a plausible account of their disagreement without demonstrating that it corresponds to the empirical relation.
        
    
- Neither achievement restores the interrupted interaction.
    
- Their independence distinguishes this configuration from ordinary pattern generation, where empirical determination and symbolic design progressively support and constrain one another.
    

#### 6. Backgrounding and progression between stages

- Empirical stabilisation and provisional explanation jointly make it possible to background discordance.
    
    - Empirical stability warrants continued reliance on coordinated outcomes.
        
    - The plausible explanation permits the disagreement to remain provisionally accounted for without requiring further investigation.
        
    
- Inquiry can consequently shift its focus to assessing the model as a whole.
    
- Successful assessment provides further grounds for retaining the discordant elements.
    
- Discordance arising during model assessment can similarly be backgrounded through subsequent fruitful use.
    
- Backgrounding allows scientific development to continue without restoring the locally interrupted empirical–symbolic interaction.
    

#### 7. Persistent discordance

- Persistent discordance involves three conditions:
    
    - Empirical: discordant outcomes exhibit sufficiently stable relations under relevant variation.
        
    - Symbolic: a plausible explanation is available but remains empirically undemonstrated.
        
    - Developmental: the discrepancy is backgrounded through progression to further model assessment or use.
        
    
- These conditions distinguish persistent discordance from operational or attributive resolution and from disagreement that continues to obstruct inquiry.
    
- Persistence therefore does not imply inactivity: empirical coordination and symbolic explanation continue to work independently.
    

#### 8. Implication for pattern articulation

- The empirical–symbolic interaction underlying pattern generation need not be completed uniformly throughout a developing model.
    
- Some features can remain discordant while the interaction continues elsewhere.
    
- Empirical stabilisation and symbolic explanation make continued development possible without resolving the local interruption.
    
- Pattern articulation can therefore proceed through the independent functioning of empirical and symbolic activities where their ordinary interaction remains disrupted.
