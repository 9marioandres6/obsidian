Here's why many scholars resent Heidegger: it's the result of two contradictory issues:

1. They don't understand him (or his core concepts).
2. His work appears unavoidable (or fundamentally essential).

Indeed, there are two more premises that complete the _aporia_:

3. Any scholar who doesn't understand an essential thinker is a bad professional.
4. I am not a bad professional.

Based on the cases I know, premises 3 and 4 are often held to be true; what I want to point out is that most of them choose the easiest path, namely, attacking premise 2 rather than premise 1.
