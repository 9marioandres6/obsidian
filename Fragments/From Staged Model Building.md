The iteration involved becomes especially revealing when attention is directed to pattern articulation. During the generative stage, while still in formation, patterns appear to scientists as post rem, that is, as an ordering to be achieved through the coordinative work of model design and multimodal determination. In the delimitation stage, patterns take on an in re status, insofar as they are tracked as instantiated across diverse systems, experimental contexts or representational frameworks, without yet being treated as autonomous or fully independent. Finally, in the stabilisation phase, patterns come to function ante rem: they are taken to be prior not only to particular modelling choices but also to the devices that instantiate them, which are subsequently interpreted as realisations of an already determinate structure. This achieved independence enables their reuse and extension, especially in the generation of further patterns. Seen through this lens, ante rem patterns serve as scaffolding for post rem constructions, compensating for their initial underdetermination and rendering epistemic iteration internally coherent.
