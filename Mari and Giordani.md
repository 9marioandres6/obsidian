#### Don Ihde – _Instrumental Realism_ (1991), _Expanding Hermeneutics_ (1998)

- Ihde explicitly links **phenomenology and philosophy of science**.
- He analyzes how **instruments mediate phenomena** — not as distortions but as _amplifications_ and _transformations_ of how the world appears.
- For him, there is no world “behind” measurement; measurement is a _technologically mediated way of encountering the world_.
- Quote (_Instrumental Realism_, p. 17):
    > “Scientific instruments are not barriers between us and the world but the very means by which new dimensions of the world become available.”

This is the most direct contemporary phenomenological stance parallel to Mari & Giordani’s realist interpretation.

If we reinterpret their scheme through **Don Ihde’s phenomenology of instrumentation**, several shifts occur — not toward antirealism, but toward a **phenomenological realism of disclosure** rather than correspondence.

* _No external truthmaker_ — truth lies in the coherence and intersubjective stability of disclosure.
* The world becomes richer and, in this case, more refined and accurate — not better mirrored.
* While for M&G quantities exist independently but are accessed through theory, for Ihde reality is _technologically disclosed_; new aspects of being emerge with instruments (e.g., atoms seen through electron microscopes).

| Mari & Giordani                                                                                             | Ihde                                                                                                                                      |
| ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Measurement sentences are _true_ because there exist effective quantities in the world that make them true. | Measurement sentences are _ways in which the world appears_ through technological mediation; truth is _disclosive_, not representational. |
| Instruments and models _represent_ aspects of the world.                                                    | Instruments _transform_ and _amplify_ phenomena — they _constitute_ new appearances of the world.                                         |
| Reference is successful because it points to real quantities.                                               | Reference is _performative_: the act of measurement _brings forth_ a phenomenon as measurable.                                            |



#### How this comments or modifies Mari & Giordani

Mari and Giordani describe the relation between model and reality mostly in _semantic_ terms: the measurement statement is true _because_ a real effective quantity exists that makes it true.  
Your proposal reframes this ontological correspondence **structurally**:

> Instead of saying that the statement is made true by an effective quantity, we say that the measurement process succeeds when the formal structure of the model is **isomorphic** to the empirical structure instantiated in the measurement operation.

That changes the explanatory focus:
- From **reference** (“the model term refers to a real property”)
- To **structural adequacy** (“the model preserves the relational form of empirical operations”).

This fits well with what Mari and Giordani call “the structural articulation of measurement” (§2.3–2.4), but you’re giving that structure a **methodological meaning** rather than an ontological one.

#### Reformulating their key relation

Instead of

> length(a) ≈₍C,T₎ 12.3 cm  
> we would write (conceptually, not as a formal equation):

> **Mₛ ≅ Mₑ**  
> where the isomorphism _φ_ : Mₑ → Mₛ preserves the relevant quantitative relations.

That means:
- The _ratio_ relations observed empirically (e.g., two rods compare as 1 : 2 in length) are mirrored in the model’s numerical structure (e.g., 10 cm : 20 cm = 1 : 2).
- The measurement operation is successful when these relational invariants hold across the mapping.

#### Conceptual consequences for their theory

1. **Truthmaker replaced by structural invariance.**  
    – Instead of a metaphysically “effective quantity,” truth now rests on _preservation of structure_ between symbolic and empirical domains.
2. **Measurement as mapping, not mirroring.**  
    – Models don’t _depict_ empirical reality; they _map_ its relational form.
3. **Error and uncertainty.**  
    – Deviations are understood as _imperfect structural correspondences_; calibration adjusts the mapping φ so that structure is restored.
4. **Theory dependence.**  
    – Theoretical context (T) defines _which_ relations must be preserved — i.e., what counts as an isomorphism — integrating Mari & Giordani’s theory-dependence point.
