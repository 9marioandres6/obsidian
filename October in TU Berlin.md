Yes. Berlin differs from Helsinki because it has **no single TINT-like centre**. The most coherent arrangement would use **TU Berlin’s Institute of History and Philosophy of Science, Technology, and Literature as the formal base**, while drawing on the **Max Planck Institute for the History of Science (MPIWG)** and, more selectively, the **ICI Berlin**. TU Berlin provides the clearest philosophy-of-science host environment; MPIWG adds historical epistemology, experimental practice and material knowledge, and explicitly accommodates research visits. ## Practical priority for a one-month Berlin visit

### 1. Axel Gelfert — TU Berlin

**Models, representation, realism and scientific practice**

Gelfert would probably be the strongest principal host. The most productive discussion would concern:

- how models represent without reproducing their targets;
    
- the relation between scientific representation and epistemic function;
    
- whether your stabilised patterns constitute representations, constraints or both;
    
- how _Pragmatic Cubism_ relates to model pluralism and practice-oriented realism.
    

He provides the broadest connection across _Pattern Enactment_, _Pragmatic Cubism_ and your account of measuring systems.

### 2. Sabine Ammon — TU Berlin

**Engineering knowledge, design, artefacts and visual-material practices**

Ammon may be the closest Berlin researcher to the technological side of _Measuring Systems as Mediators_. Her work is useful for examining how designed artefacts, visualisations and material procedures participate in knowledge production.

The central question for a meeting would be whether a measuring system should be understood primarily as:

- a coordinated collection of devices;
    
- a designed epistemic artefact;
    
- a distributed process of material reasoning;
    
- or an infrastructure supporting further epistemic operations.
    

She is also relevant to your distinction between the **embodiment of a stabilised pattern in newly designed devices** and its embedding in existing technological arrangements.

### 3. Adrian Wüthrich — TU Berlin

**Experimental physics, scientific collaboration, instrumentation and concept formation**

Wüthrich is especially relevant to _Epistemic Iteration_, _Causal Circulation_ and the physical-science side of your work. His research includes collaborative observational practices in contemporary physics and the historical stabilisation of scientific concepts. Recent work with Michael Zichert, for example, argues that the concept of the virtual particle became simultaneously more stable and more polysemous after 1950. ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.02463?utm_source=chatgpt.com "The Next Generation Event Horizon Telescope Collaboration: History, Philosophy, and Culture"))

The strongest overlap concerns the way:

- instruments and representational practices coordinate access to otherwise inaccessible phenomena;
    
- distributed collaborations establish sameness-conditions;
    
- concepts stabilise while retaining internal plurality;
    
- observations circulate between empirical and symbolic settings.
    

### 4. Robert Meunier — ICI Berlin

**Experimental systems, infrastructures, validation and data-intensive biomedicine**

Meunier is the direct Berlin connection produced by session **6F**. His treatment of infrastructures as interfaces of translation closely approaches your account of measuring systems as mediators. His broader research connects practice and knowledge, data-intensive biomedical research, validation, technological assemblages and the production of new epistemic objects. ([ICI Berlin](https://www.ici-berlin.org/people/meunier/ "Meunier - ICI Berlin"))

A meeting should concentrate on the boundary between:

- **system and infrastructure**;
    
- experimental interaction and institutional circulation;
    
- mediation within a measuring arrangement and translation between research contexts;
    
- technological stabilisation and infrastructural reproduction.
    

His ICI listing specifies an affiliation for **2025–26**, so his physical presence in Berlin in October 2026 would need to be confirmed. ([ICI Berlin](https://www.ici-berlin.org/people/meunier/ "Meunier - ICI Berlin"))

### 5. Beate Krickel — TU Berlin

**Mechanisms, biological functions and organisation**

Krickel would be the strongest Berlin interlocutor for _Causal Circulation_. Her work can help test whether your account offers a genuine alternative or complement to mechanistic explanation.

The principal issues would include:

- whether causal functions belong to components, organisational relations or whole systems;
    
- how mechanisms are individuated across different empirical and symbolic descriptions;
    
- whether sensitivity analysis reveals mechanisms or merely identifies dependency structures;
    
- how your node–pattern distinction relates to component–organisation accounts.
    

Her relevance is more concentrated than Gelfert’s or Ammon’s, but the overlap with the causal paper is precise.

### 6. Etienne Benson — MPIWG

**Knowledge systems, field sciences, tracking technologies, data and environmental inquiry**

Benson is the strongest active MPIWG contact for _Robustness Outside the Laboratory_. His work examines tracking technologies, environmental sciences, quantitative approaches to landscapes, databases and data communities. He currently directs the MPIWG Department of Knowledge Systems and Collective Life and has held an honorary professorship at TU Berlin since 2026. ([mpiwg-berlin.mpg.de](https://www.mpiwg-berlin.mpg.de/de/people/ebenson "Etienne Benson | MPIWG"))

His cases could extend your framework beyond laboratory and industrial measurement by examining:

- how environmental phenomena become tractable through distributed data systems;
    
- how field observations are standardised without eliminating contextual variation;
    
- how databases coordinate heterogeneous empirical pathways;
    
- how technological systems transform landscapes into measurable scientific objects.
    

He would also be an important institutional contact for gaining access to the MPIWG research community.

### 7. Hans-Jörg Rheinberger — MPIWG emeritus

**Experimental systems, epistemic things, technical objects and iteration**

Rheinberger is perhaps the most conceptually important Berlin researcher for your work, even though he is less plausible as the formal organiser of a visit. His account of experimental systems directly concerns the movement between uncertain objects of inquiry and stabilised technical objects that subsequently structure further research.

A discussion would be highly valuable for clarifying the differences between:

- his experimental systems and your measuring systems;
    
- epistemic things and your patterns;
    
- technical objects and stabilised patterns embodied in devices;
    
- experimental iteration and your larger circuit of generation, delimitation and stabilisation.
    

The connection is substantial, but your account should not be presented merely as an application of Rheinberger. Your framework gives greater emphasis to multimodal determination, robustness relations and the circulation between empirical and symbolic ambits.

### 8. Friedrich Steinle — TU Berlin emeritus

**Exploratory experimentation and scientific concept formation**

Steinle is especially relevant to _Epistemic Iteration_ and _Pattern Enactment_. His work on exploratory experimentation shows how experiments can organise a domain and generate concepts before a settled theoretical framework is available.

A meeting could clarify:

- whether your generation stage corresponds to exploratory experimentation;
    
- how patterns acquire conceptual articulation through experimental organisation;
    
- whether concept stabilisation requires convergence;
    
- how exploratory and theory-guided experimentation interact over time.
    

He retired from his TU Berlin professorship in 2024, so this would be an availability-dependent meeting rather than part of the formal structure of the visit.

## Additional high-value researchers

### Ursula Klein — MPIWG

**Paper tools, chemistry, material-symbolic operations and technoscience**

Klein is highly relevant to your insistence that scientific work cannot be divided neatly into concrete material operations and abstract symbolic representation. Her analyses of paper tools show how symbolic inscriptions can function as operative instruments within material scientific practices. She is currently listed as a visiting senior research fellow at MPIWG. ([mpiwg-berlin.mpg.de](https://www.mpiwg-berlin.mpg.de/de/personen/mitarbeiter-innen "Personen | MPIWG"))

She would be especially useful for _Pattern Enactment_ and the empirical–symbolic architecture of your robustness framework.

### Lorraine Daston — MPIWG emerita

**Objectivity, observation, scientific data and epistemic practices**

Daston could provide a broader historical test of your ideas of stabilisation, permanence and backgrounding. Her current MPIWG profile identifies her work with moral and natural orders, the history of scientific data and the cognitive practices of science. ([mpiwg-berlin.mpg.de](https://www.mpiwg-berlin.mpg.de/de/personen/mitarbeiter-innen "Personen | MPIWG"))

The strongest connection concerns how historically cultivated practices become so authoritative that their contingent formation recedes from view.

### Simon Brausch — MPIWG

**Validation, reliability and methodological assessment**

Brausch is a junior but unusually precise contact for _Managing Discordance_. His current project examines technical tools used to assess the methodological quality of clinical trials and is situated within MPIWG work on biomedical validation. ([mpiwg-berlin.mpg.de](https://www.mpiwg-berlin.mpg.de/de/personen/mitarbeiter-innen "Personen | MPIWG"))

He could be useful for discussing:

- the distinction between reliability and robustness;
    
- how evaluative tools render heterogeneous studies comparable;
    
- whether methodological assessment resolves discordance or stabilises its management;
    
- how standards become embedded in evidence appraisal.
    

### Olaf Müller — Humboldt University

**Scientific realism, naturalism and philosophy of physics**

Müller is a secondary rather than central match. He could be useful for the metaphysical and methodological background of _Pragmatic Cubism_ and for your separate naturalism project, especially concerning the relation between realism, scientific practice and philosophical naturalism.
